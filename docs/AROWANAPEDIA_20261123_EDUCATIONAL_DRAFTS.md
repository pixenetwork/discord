## Draft 01 — Arowanas: six true species, and the African cousin

**Status:** UNPUBLISHED / NEEDS EDITORIAL AND SCIENCE REVIEW

**Discord-ready text:**

**Fact of the day:** Six living *true arowana* species are conventionally placed in two genera—*Scleropages* and *Osteoglossum*. The so-called African arowana, *Heterotis niloticus*, is a related bonytongue with different feeding and breeding biology. An aquarium trade name is not a taxonomic species.

Read further: https://aquapedia.app/arowanapedia/

## Draft 04 — Batik arowana: a real described taxon

**Status:** UNPUBLISHED / NEEDS EDITORIAL AND SCIENCE REVIEW

**Discord-ready text:**

**Species spotlight:** *Scleropages inscriptus*, often called Batik arowana, was described from Myanmar's Tanintharyi/Tenasserim system and is distinguished by intricate maze-like scale/head patterns. It is not simply an Asian red or golden color grade. Check current legal treatment before discussing international trade.

## Draft 05 — Jardini: a Northern Saratoga, not a Golden Crossback

## Draft 07 — Silver arowana: not the Asian Yellow Tail Silver

**Status:** UNPUBLISHED / NEEDS EDITORIAL AND SCIENCE REVIEW

**Discord-ready text:**

**Identity tip:** The South American silver arowana is *Osteoglossum bicirrhosum*. Asian fish marketed as Yellow Tail Silver are a color/phenotype group within *Scleropages formosus*. They are not the same species, and both need substantial, securely covered adult housing.

Read further: https://aquapedia.app/arowanapedia/silver-arowana/

**Research evidence:**

## Draft 08 — Black arowana: another South American species

**Status:** UNPUBLISHED / NEEDS EDITORIAL AND SCIENCE REVIEW

**Discord-ready text:**

**Species spotlight:** *Osteoglossum ferreirai* is a distinct black arowana species associated with the Rio Negro region. Conservation literature distinguishes it genetically from the silver arowana, and both are low-fecundity male mouthbrooders. Ask for credible legal-source documentation, not unverified captive-bred claims.

## Draft 11 — Why an arowana microchip is not a strain-gene test

**Status:** UNPUBLISHED / NEEDS EDITORIAL AND SCIENCE REVIEW

**Discord-ready text:**

**Documentation lesson:** An official coded microchip helps identify an individual fish under a legal provenance system. It is not DNA evidence that a named color variety breeds true or was created by a particular breeder. Compare tag records, authorized breeder documents, and an independently verifiable family pedigree.

Read further: https://aquapedia.app/arowanapedia/breeders/registered-farms/
