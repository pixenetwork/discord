# Plecopedia focused primary-source cross-checks — 23 October 2026

All conclusions in this file refer to **the cited document and its public abstract**, not to verified specimens, aquarium breeding lineages, supplier authenticity, or licensing. No article is reproduced.

## Three targeted groups

- **L267 / published striped Ancistrus:** [Neuhaus and colleagues, Journal of Fish Biology, DOI 10.1111/jfb.15637](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfb.15637), first published 20 December 2023 in advance of its 2024 journal issue, describes a striped bushynose catfish from the Palcazú drainage and explicitly recognizes its former aquarium-trade code **L267**. The DOI is directly relevant to the historical L-number association; it does **not** establish the pedigree or identity of any live aquarium specimen. Affected Aquapedia proposals: `anc-l267-alias` and `anc-yanesha`.
- **Ancistrus leoni and A. patronus:** [de Souza, Taphorn and Armbruster, Zootaxa 4552(1), DOI 10.11646/zootaxa.4552.1.1](https://doi.org/10.11646/zootaxa.4552.1.1) includes both as new species in a regional systematic revision. This supports using the paper as a taxonomic citation, not as proof of captive origins, precise grade traits or aquarium genotype. Affected proposals: `anc-leoni` and `anc-patronus`.
- **L333 documented captive breeding study:** [Reis and colleagues, Aquaculture International, DOI 10.1007/s10499-024-01720-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10499-024-01720-z), accepted in 2024 and published in the 2025 volume, explicitly reports captive breeding work with L333 *Hypancistrus* fish. The study supports **that the authors reported controlled breeding in their study**, not a universal result for all L333 stocks or a genetically authenticated strain. Affected proposal: `study-l333-captive`.
